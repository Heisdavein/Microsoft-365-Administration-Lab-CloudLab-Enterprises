# 07. Troubleshooting

Not everything worked first time. Four problems came up during the build, and investigating each one taught me how the platform behaves.

## Quick reference

| # | Problem | Cause | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Locked out of the tenant after enabling Conditional Access | Admin account not excluded; laptop not enrolled so treated as non-compliant | Recovered via account recovery, then created two break-glass accounts excluded from policy |
| 2 | DLP kept flagging test emails | Policy working as designed and generating incidents against the admin account | Reviewed incident reports in Purview, temporary exception during testing, removed afterwards |
| 3 | Intune policies reported Not Applicable | Device enrolled as personal, not corporate | Removed enrolment and re-enrolled with the work account |
| 4 | Unexpected access to a Finance subfolder | Subfolder did not carry the permissions I expected | Broke inheritance on the subfolder, set permissions, re-tested |

## Challenge 1: Conditional Access locked me out of my own tenant

**What happened.** I created a policy requiring compliant devices for Microsoft 365 access. My own Global Administrator account was in scope. When the policy took effect, I lost access to the tenant. The laptop I was using was not enrolled in Intune, so Microsoft treated it as non-compliant and blocked sign-in.

**Resolution.** I recovered access through Microsoft’s account recovery process, which took roughly thirty minutes. Straight afterwards I created **two emergency (break-glass) administrator accounts** with long complex passwords, excluded them from every Conditional Access policy, and stored the credentials securely. They are for emergencies only, never daily administration.

**Lesson.** Security controls can be as dangerous to administrators as to attackers when misconfigured. Break-glass accounts should exist before restrictive Conditional Access policies are enabled. Report-only mode is the safe way to see the effect first.

## Challenge 2: My DLP policy started blocking my own test emails

**What happened.** To test DLP I emailed a fake credit card number from the tenant to a Gmail account. It was blocked, as expected. But later test messages without sensitive data were also flagged.

**Investigation.** Rather than change the policy, I opened the DLP incident reports in Microsoft Purview. The policy was behaving as designed: it had generated a security incident after detecting sensitive information and associated that activity with my administrator account.

**Resolution.** I created a temporary exception for my admin account so I could finish testing, and removed it afterwards.

**Lesson.** DLP is also an investigation tool. Each violation creates reports, alerts and incident records, and knowing how to review them matters as much as creating the policy.

## Challenge 3: Intune policies refused to apply

**What happened.** Configuration profiles and compliance policies reported **Not Applicable**. I checked the assignments several times and they were correct.

**Cause.** The test Windows device had been enrolled as a **personal** device. Some Intune policies are designed for corporate-owned devices and do not apply to personally enrolled ones.

**Resolution.** I removed the existing enrolment and enrolled again with my work account through Windows Settings. The policies applied successfully after the new enrolment.

**Lesson.** The configuration can be correct while the environment does not meet the conditions for the policy to apply. Device ownership changes how Intune behaves.


## Challenge 4: SharePoint permissions did not behave as expected

**What happened.** I had broken inheritance on the Budget Planning library and granted access to Finance managers only. Later I created a subfolder and uploaded documents. Signed in as a standard Finance employee, I could still open documents that should have been restricted.

**Cause.** The new subfolder had inherited different permissions from the ones I expected, so it was not restricting access as I intended.

**Resolution.** I broke inheritance on the subfolder itself, assigned the appropriate permissions manually and tested again. Only authorised Finance managers could then access the documents.

**Lesson.** Permissions need verifying whenever new folders or libraries are created. I now test with different user accounts instead of assuming inheritance behaves as expected.


## Approach

When something does not behave as expected I check the evidence before changing settings: which portal records the activity, what the policy status says, which account is affected, and whether the environment meets the policy’s conditions. Testing with a second user account shows whether a result is real or an artefact of the admin account.
